How Solum works

  1. A deed gets pledged. Someone locks a property deed as collateral for a loan on an unrelated platform, on Ethereum.
  2. Attestcoin proves it, trustlessly. No oracle, no partnership — Solum verifies the real transaction with a cryptographic proof.
  3. You apply here. Before approving your mortgage, Solum checks that same deed against every pledge it has verified.
  4. Clean or conflicting. Approved instantly if it's clean; declined — and told why — if it's already pledged elsewhere.

FAQ

Do I need a crypto wallet to use Solum?
No. Sign in with just an email — Solum creates and funds a loan account for you behind the scenes. You never see a wallet address, gas, or a seed phrase.
How does Solum know my deed isn't pledged elsewhere?
Through the Attestcoin Protocol: a cryptographic proof that a specific transaction happened on another chain. No oracle, no data-sharing agreement with the other platform — Solum verifies it directly.
What happens if my deed is already pledged?
Your application is declined instantly, with the reason stated in plain English. Nothing is ever silently approved.
Is this real money?
No — this is a testnet build for the BUIDL CTC 2026 Fall hackathon on Creditcoin. All values, deeds, and platforms in the demo are illustrative.
Which chains does this run on?
Ethereum Sepolia for the collateral-pledge registry, and Creditcoin's CC3 testnet for the mortgage decision itself — connected by the Attestcoin Protocol.
What actually happens to the loan money after approval?
An approved application disburses testnet tCTC to your Solum account — 1 tCTC per 100,000 of loan amount you entered, illustrative only. You can see the balance and withdraw it to any address you hold, so the loan isn't just a status flag.
How is my repayment schedule calculated?
Illustrative only: every approved loan uses a flat demo term (12 months) and a flat demo APR (5%) to compute a monthly payment, total interest, and payoff date — the same way a real amortization breakdown reads, but with fixed demo assumptions instead of real underwriting.
